3.2

Recording the flow

MaterialsLaborOverheadFinished and sold

$46,000 leaves the storeroom

  • (1) Harborlight buys $50,000 of materials on account: debit Raw Materials, credit Accounts Payable
  • (2) Requisitions issue $46,000: $40,000 direct to Work in Process, $6,000 indirect (glue, fasteners) to Manufacturing Overhead
  • Still in the storeroom: $8,000 + $50,000 − $46,000 = $12,000

Payroll splits two ways too

  • (3) March factory payroll is $92,000
  • Time tickets trace $80,000 to specific jobs (4,000 hours × $20): direct labor. The other $12,000 (supervisors, maintenance) is indirect
  • Debit Work in Process $80,000 and Manufacturing Overhead $12,000; credit Salaries and Wages Payable $92,000

Actual in, applied out

  • Every actual factory cost is a debit: $6,000 + $12,000 + $16,000 = $34,000
  • (5) Jobs get overhead at the rate, never from the bills: 4,000 hours × $8 = $32,000. Debit Work in Process, credit Manufacturing Overhead
  • What's left is a $2,000 debit balance: actual beat applied, so overhead is underapplied (section 3.4)

Selling and admin skip the factory

  • (6) March selling and administrative costs: sales salaries, advertising and depreciation on office equipment
  • Period costs skip the factory: debit expense accounts, never Work in Process or Manufacturing Overhead
  • All $22,000 is expensed in March, whether or not a single sign sold

Finished, then sold

  • (7) Jobs costing $150,000 are finished: debit Finished Goods, credit Work in Process. That's cost of goods manufactured
  • (8) Jobs costing $140,000 are sold for $210,000: debit Cost of Goods Sold, credit Finished Goods (and debit Accounts Receivable, credit Sales)

3.4

Under- and overapplied overhead

Debit or credit balanceClose to COGSAllocate

Debit balance or credit balance?

  • Actual overhead piles up as debits: $34,000
  • Applied overhead leaves as credits: $32,000
Debit balance · actual > appliedUnderapplied: jobs were undercosted, so cost must be added
Credit balance · applied > actualOverapplied: jobs were overcosted, so cost must be taken out

Two ways to clear $2,000

  • Method 1, the usual choice: close the whole $2,000 to Cost of Goods Sold, which rises from $140,000 to $142,000
  • Method 2 splits it in proportion to the overhead applied that sits in each account at month end: 15% / 25% / 60% → $300 / $500 / $1,200
