4.1

Process vs job-order costing

Departments, not jobsProcessing departmentsConversion cost

Jobs, or a flow?

Job-orderDifferent orders: costs collected by job, on a job cost sheet
ProcessIdentical units: costs collected by department, then spread over the units
BothSame accounts and flow: Raw Materials → Work in Process → Finished Goods → Cost of Goods Sold

Department by department

  • A processing department adds materials, labor or overhead, and treats every gallon exactly the same
  • The sauce moves on to Bottling in a fixed order, and its cost goes with it
  • Conversion cost = direct labor + manufacturing overhead: the cost of turning materials into product

4.3

Equivalent units

Partly finished unitsMaterials vs conversionWeighted-average

Two halves = one whole

  • At month end, some gallons are only partly blended. They can't count as whole gallons, and they can't be ignored
  • Two half-done gallons hold the same work as one finished gallon: 1 equivalent unit
  • Equivalent units = partly finished units × percent complete

Pour the partial work together

Each tank = 1,000 gallons · 2,000 equivalent units
8,000 gallons × 25% = 2,000 equivalent units. Press Pour together to see why.

Done + what's in the tank

  • Every gallon that left Blending counts as a whole unit: 34,000
  • Add the work sitting in the 6,000 gallons of ending inventory: materials 80% done, conversion 50% done
  • Equivalent units: 38,800 for materials, 37,000 for conversion. Beginning inventory's percent complete never enters

Cost per equivalent unit

Beginning + added÷ equivalent unitsBy category

Pool the cost, then divide

  • Weighted-average pools beginning inventory's cost with May's added cost, for each category
  • Then divides each pool by its own equivalent units: $97,000 ÷ 38,800 = $2.50; $55,500 ÷ 37,000 = $1.50
  • A finished gallon carries all of both: $4.00

4.6

Operation costing

BatchesShared operationsHybrid

Half job-order, half process

  • Materials differ a lot between batches, so they're traced to each batch, like job-order costing
  • Every pair gets the same cutting and stitching, so conversion is one rate per operation, like process costing
  • That mix is operation costing: a hybrid for batches of different products sharing the same operations
