Chapter 2

Job-Order Costing: Calculating Unit Product Costs

How a shop that builds every order differently figures out what each order cost: trace materials and labor to the job, then apply overhead with a rate set before the year begins.

What you'll learn

  • 2.1Job-order costing. When it's used, the job cost sheet, materials requisitions and time tickets
  • 2.2Predetermined overhead rate. Allocation bases, why the rate is set in advance, and the four-step calculation
  • 2.3Applying overhead. Predetermined rate × the job's actual use of the base
  • 2.4Total and unit job cost. Adding up the job cost sheet and choosing a good allocation base
  • 2.5Multiple overhead rates. Departmental rates and how they change a job's cost and price
  • 2.6Job cost sheets and the statements. Work in process, finished goods, cost of goods sold, and under- or overapplied overhead
