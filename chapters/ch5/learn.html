Every candle counts

Brightwick Candle Co. has $40,000 of bills before it sells a single candle. How many candles until it stops losing money, and what happens when it changes the price?

5.1

Contribution margin

Unit CMCM ratioProfit equation

Where does the $25 go?

  • Each candle sells for $25
  • $15 goes to variable costs. The other $10 is the contribution margin: $25 − $15
  • That $10 first covers the $40,000 of monthly fixed expenses, then becomes profit

Fill the $40,000 first

  • Each candle pours $10 into the fixed-cost tank
  • At 4,000 candles the tank is full: that's break-even
  • After that, each candle adds $10 straight to profit

Sell more, watch profit

Profit = $10 × 5,000 − $40,000 = $10,000.

40¢ of every sales dollar

  • CM ratio = $10 ÷ $25 = 40%; variable expense ratio = $15 ÷ $25 = 60%
  • $20,000 more sales × 40% = $8,000 more contribution margin
  • Fixed expenses don't move, so net operating income rises the full $8,000. Profit = CM ratio × sales − fixed expenses
5.2

Operating leverage

CM ÷ NOISmall push, big swing

+10% sales, +50% profit

  • At 5,000 candles: contribution margin $50,000, net operating income $10,000
  • Sell 10% more (5,500 candles) and profit jumps 50%, to $15,000
  • Degree of operating leverage = CM ÷ NOI = 5: profit moves five times as fast as sales

Same profit today, different risk

Brightwick's high fixed costs give it more leverage: profit rises faster on the way up, and falls faster on the way down.
5.3

Break-even and margin of safety

UnitsSales dollarsThe cushion

Two ways to break even

  • Break-even units = fixed expenses ÷ unit CM
  • Break-even sales = fixed expenses ÷ CM ratio
  • Same point, two ways. At break-even, contribution margin exactly equals fixed expenses

The cushion above break-even

  • Brightwick sells $125,000 a month
  • Break-even is $100,000
  • Margin of safety = $125,000 − $100,000 = $25,000: sales could fall 20% before it starts losing money

5.5

What-if changes

Incremental analysisPrice, cost, volumeCommissions

Only count what changes

  • Change in NOI = change in contribution margin − change in fixed expenses
  • A price cut lowers unit CM, so volume must rise enough to make up for it
  • Turning a fixed cost into a variable one lowers risk: less to cover before break-even

5.6

The CVP graph

Four linesBreak-even pointProfit graph

Draw it in four steps

  • Fixed expenses: a flat line at $40,000
  • Total expenses: start at $40,000 and rise $15 per candle
  • Sales: start at $0 and rise $25 per candle
  • Where they cross is break-even. Left of it is loss; right of it is profit
